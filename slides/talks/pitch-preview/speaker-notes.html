Pitch speaker notes

Open preview deck · Working script in slide order. Target 8:15, leaving time for transitions and recovery within the ten-minute slot.

1. Title

Briefly introduce the project and team, then advance to the bacterial question. Keep this introduction within the existing opening time.

2. What should we test next? 0:00–0:50

“Imagine a researcher studying a bacterial community. They already have a compound and a set of strains. Will that compound inhibit the pathogen while sparing the commensals? What experiment would answer that question?”

Start with the plate alone. Reveal the bacterial groups, then the compound and proposed inhibitory effect, then the experiment question. The grey pathogen illustrates the hypothesis, not an observed result. Compound discovery and strain selection happen upstream.

3. The missing measurement 0:50–2:00

“Some compound–strain relationships already have measurements. For this question, the connection we need is missing. That is where we want to help: turning an unanswered question into a justified experiment.”

Reveal the question and X/Y labels, then focus on the missing connection. Assume an up-to-date knowledge base for this conceptual story. If evidence already answers a question, return that evidence with its sources; the researcher decides whether another experiment is worthwhile.

4. Evidence and templates → proposal 2:00–3:00

“Measurements for related compounds provide context. Papers support those measurements. Separately, protocol templates provide the assay structure. We use both to propose an assay, concentration series, controls and replicates—with a rationale and supporting references.”

Follow the four reveals: compound–strain links, supporting papers, protocol templates, proposal. Templates are not necessarily the original protocols used in the linked papers. This is a conceptual bacterial workflow; the current agent uses kinase/IC50 retrieval, while bacterial evidence and MIC execution are separate components. Species-level evidence does not establish a measurement for every strain.

5. Can the model design the missing experiment? 3:00–4:15

Setup: “We test this by hiding a known measurement and its source papers. Each model gets the same remaining evidence and protocol steps with unfilled parameters. We ask it to design the experiment that would fill the gap.”

Criteria: “We assess three things: does the assay answer the question, are the steps sound and complete, and are the numbers justified and consistent?”

Example: “One case asks for an experiment to measure how compound CHEMBL4278808 inhibits the kinase haspin. The task is to design the procedure and justify its settings, not recall the hidden potency.”

Chart: “This small pilot compares five models on three kinase or cell-based cases. Each criterion earns zero to two points per case, for a maximum of eighteen. These are provisional model-assisted review scores, not experimental success rates.”

Optional concrete example: “One DeepSeek response told the researcher to prepare eleven different concentrations, but the same response listed ten in its experiment settings. A person would need to resolve that disagreement before using the proposal.”

If asked about the inputs: The seven step cards are manually generalized benchmark scaffolds. Each supplies an action, template reference, parameter slots and, where applicable, prerequisites. The analysis card is reviewer-authored. They substitute for raw protocol text, numeric defaults and quotes; this does not test automatic extraction of steps from papers. Retained comparison measurements intentionally still contain numbers.

If asked about confidence: One response per model per case; three selected, correlated cases; provisional, unblinded and uncalibrated Codex grading, without expert validation. Generation used fixed packets, without live retrieval. This does not establish a robust model ranking, a benefit over deterministic template filling, improved performance due to the knowledge graph, or bacterial end-to-end performance.

6. Evidence walkthrough 4:15–7:15

Spend 30 seconds on the question and supplied input, 60 seconds on retained evidence and procedural steps, 45 seconds on the withheld record and recorded model output, and 45 seconds on the separate MIC execution example.

“Here is the evidence the model received, and here is the proposal it produced. We can inspect the experimental choice, the procedure and the numbers. Separately, our bacterial execution component turns a supplied proposal into a plate layout and robot protocol.”

The MIC example starts from a hand-written four-species proposal. It is not the execution of the model output just shown. Describe simulation as simulation; do not imply a completed wet-lab experiment. Use the pre-opened recorded artifacts.

7. Propose an experiment—and show why 7:15–8:15

“The output we want is an assay, concentration range, controls and procedure, with supporting evidence for the researcher to review. Here is where that fits in the broader research process.”

Existing answer: “Start with the researcher’s question and check the knowledge base. If evidence answers it, return the result with its sources. The researcher can still choose to run a confirmatory experiment.”

Missing measurement: “If the measurement is missing, propose an experiment. The researcher reviews the design and decides whether to run it.”

Results feed back: “A potential integration is to add the measured results and their sources back into the knowledge base. That experiment then becomes evidence a future question can use.”

Upstream connection: “We could also connect hypothesis refinement upstream—helping a researcher sharpen an existing idea into a testable question.”

Show both implemented branches and the researcher/lab handoff together at the start. Reveal the feedback loop on the next click, then hypothesis refinement on the final click. The accented region, labeled “Our system · implemented,” includes both returning an existing answer and proposing an experiment when a measurement is missing. Solid internal connections indicate these implemented paths. The muted dotted connections indicate hypothesis refinement and measured-result ingestion. This distinction is about implementation status, not which branch the benchmark evaluates. Researcher review and laboratory execution sit outside the box; the solid handoff does not imply autonomous execution. This is a workflow overview, not a replay of an implemented end-to-end bacterial run. Ingestion should preserve assay conditions, units, strain identity and provenance, including negative results; a proposed or simulated result is not a measured result. The bacterial execution demonstration still begins with a hand-written proposal. An unresolved compound or organism is not evidence that its measurement is absent.
